# Botlien prototype: three fixes to the Costs screen

Same source of truth as the original brief: branch `origin/owner-formula-discretion`,
commit `07ea9aa7`. Edit `prototype/src/botlien.part.html`, then
`cd prototype/src && node build.cjs`. Never edit the generated file.

**The Costs screen is built and it is close to right. This is a surgical patch, not a
revision.** Do not rebuild a section, do not restyle anything, do not touch the tiles, the
brand table, the guards or the small print. Three changes, nothing else.

---

## 1. One shared vertical scale across every timing strip

This is the actual bug and it is the same failure the floor map had: the picture argues
against the sentence printed underneath it.

Each strip is currently normalised to its own maximum. The result:

| strip | total lost | how it draws |
|---|---|---|
| Order picking | **29.3 hours** | bars vary in height, peak bracket 11.1h |
| Floor cleaning | **1.7 hours** | every bar in the window solid at full height, bracket 0.5h |

So the strip representing a trivial, evenly spread problem is visually heavier and denser
than the strip representing a problem seventeen times larger. Anyone scanning the page
concludes the floor cleaners are the disaster, which is the exact opposite of what the
text below them says.

**Fix.** Compute the ceiling **once per page**, from the tallest single-hour stall figure
found across every strip, and scale all strips against that one number. Floor cleaning
should then render as a barely visible sliver, because that is the truth.

Three things to hold while doing it:

- **No minimum bar height, no clamping.** A near-zero hour must draw as near-zero. An
  always-visible floor reintroduces the same lie in a smaller form.
- **Keep each strip's own bracket and figure.** The bracket marks that strip's worst two
  hours, which stays a real finding even when the whole strip is small. Only the vertical
  scale is shared, not the bracket.
- **Name the scale in the legend** so it can be read rather than guessed. After "bracket
  marks the worst two hours", add the page ceiling, for example `tallest bar = 6.2h`. Bar
  height is one hour of stall time; the bracket figure is a two-hour total. Those are
  different quantities and the legend should not let a reader confuse them.

## 2. Take the design rationale off the customer's screen

The section currently opens with:

> Your robots lost 31.0 hours to stalls this period. Everyone can read a clock, so this is
> on the hour of the day rather than a spot on a floor plan, and it needs only a timestamp,
> which your export already carries. Peaks are found inside one kind of work, never across
> the fleet: a night scrubber scored against the day shift's picking peak would read as
> permanently idle.

Everything after the first sentence is our internal reasoning about how we built the chart.
The customer never saw a floor plan and does not care that we rejected one. Explaining our
own methodology unprompted reads as defending a choice nobody challenged.

**Replace the whole paragraph with the first sentence and a pointer:**

> Your robots lost **31.0 hours** to stalls this period. Here is when.

The "peaks are found inside one kind of work" reasoning does not move, it goes. The
separate strips already demonstrate it without saying it. If it has to survive anywhere, it
belongs in the small print under a strip, never above the section.

## 3. Confirm §0 actually shipped

The **"A person vs your robots"** card on the Dashboard must now read:

```
YOUR ROBOTS   $17,648.00
              $16,700.00 leased + $948.00 of your team's time keeping them running
Your robots cost $22,326.40 less this period
```

If it still shows `$16,700.00` and `$23,274.40 less`, that edit was missed. It is the single
most valuable change in the original brief, because it is the one that makes the product's
largest claim survive an operator poking at it.

## What not to touch

- The three tiles, their figures, and their measured-versus-estimated captions.
- The small print naming the six-minute assumption.
- The small print explaining that the share of running time inside the bracket is assumed
  rather than measured. **That sentence is correct and it stays.**
- The brand table, its two guards, and the "Fetch rests on one machine, Freight runner" note.
- The flat-reading branch on floor cleaning ("no shift to go and watch here"). A dashboard
  that can say nothing-here is worth more than one that always finds something.
- Colour. Still monochrome, still no status colours anywhere.

## One note for later, not for this patch

When this section is ported into the served app (`src/`), the running-time caveat can be
removed. `utilization_rollups` already stores `active_ms` per hourly bucket, so per-hour
running time is genuinely measured there and the rate stops being an assumption. Do **not**
remove it in the prototype, where it is true.
