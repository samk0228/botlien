## Why RaaS operators lead, not lenders

RaaS operators can answer the question that used to require three separate tracks to triangulate: **does financial telemetry (task completion, quota performance, cash flow) actually matter enough to an operator's own business that they'd pay for it directly.** That's no longer an inferred answer from lender and manufacturer conversations, it's the direct question this track exists to answer, and it resolves "who pays" outright, the operator does, for their own tool.

## Track 1 — RaaS Operators (primary, direct customer)

This is the track that matters most now. Frame every conversation here as fleet-operator-to-fleet-operator, not vendor-to-prospect, and lead with the financial-telemetry pitch (cash flow visibility, quota tracking), not collateral monitoring.

**Standing questions:**
1. How do you currently track your own fleet's financial performance, revenue per robot, whether you're hitting contract or quota targets? In-house tooling, spreadsheets, manufacturer dashboard, nothing formal?
2. When you find out you're short of a monthly quota or target, how do you find out, and how much lead time do you actually get?
3. How do you currently finance or acquire your robot fleet, buy outright, lease, vendor financing? If financed, who's your lender or financing partner?
4. Do you extend financing or leasing terms to your own downstream customers on these robots? If so, how do you assess their risk today?
5. If a tool gave you real-time cash-flow projections and quota tracking across your fleet, would that replace something you're already paying for, or is this new spend?
6. Has your own financing rate, terms, or approval ever been affected by a lender's confidence (or lack of it) in your fleet's performance or collateral value?

**What each answer proves for the deck:**
| Question | Deck slide it feeds |
|---|---|
| Q1, Q2 | Slide 1 (Problem) — direct validation of the core "two different clocks" framing, real specifics replace directional language |
| Q3 | Slide 6 (Traction) + surfaces Track 3 lender leads directly |
| Q4 | Slide 2 (Solution) — validates the "default/credit risk one layer earlier" framing for operators who self-finance downstream |
| Q5 | **Slide 4 (Business Model)** — the direct answer to "will they pay for this," the single most load-bearing question in the pivoted model |
| Q6 | Slide 1 (Problem) — validates the collateral/performance-visibility-affects-financing-terms thesis from the operator's own experience |

## Track 3 — Lenders / Creditors (secondary, downstream expansion)

Held for the downstream motion, not an initial sales target. Relevant once a RaaS operator is a paying, active customer with real telemetry history and a lender relationship worth layering on top.

**Standing questions** (established earlier in this project, still useful once this track activates):
1. Once a loan or lease closes, do you know if the robot is still operational?
2. Have you ever taken a loss where the collateral turned out to be worth less than you thought, and you didn't know until default?
3. If you were told today a financed robot had been offline 30 days, would you actually do anything with that?
4. Where does collateral visibility rank against your other underwriting concerns?
5. Would you value verified, operator-authorized fleet performance data from a RaaS operator you finance, if it were offered to you directly?

**What each answer proves for the deck:**
| Question | Deck slide it feeds |
|---|---|
| Q1, Q2 | Slide 1 (Problem) — real war stories for the downstream/expansion narrative |
| Q3 | Slide 4 (Business Model) — validates the downstream product is actionable, not vanity monitoring |
| Q4 | Slide 6 (Traction) — signals which lender segment to prioritize once this track activates |
